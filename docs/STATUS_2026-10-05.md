**Cardiac (step 05d, done).** See `Methods/cardiac_features.md` and `Results/cardiac_analysis/report.md`.

Sample:
- 276 of 320 have a usable ECG lead.
- 218 have usable pre-task HRV.
- 228 have all three heartbeat-counting intervals valid.

Results (exploratory):
- **E1A.** Resting HRV is unrelated to baseline emotion-recognition accuracy (lnRMSSD r = .03, n = 216).
- **E1B.** HRV does not differ between the combined task and baseline GERT (p = .51).
- **E1C.** Not estimable as preregistered: a trial is too short for HRV.
- **HR and the pacer.** HR falls about 4 bpm over every paced trial; the breathing direction does not
  change that (p = .12, time-locked bins).
- **Felt arousal is not cardiac arousal.** Felt arousal tracks the prescribed change strongly
  (b = .34, p = 1e-31), but not the trial's HR change (p = .09).
- **Burnout lowers felt arousal (beta = -.16, p = .008) without lower resting HR or HRV, and without a
  smaller HR response.** Adding HR leaves the effect unchanged. This answers the thesis's open
  question: within the limits of HR, burnout looks like a change in perceiving arousal, not in
  producing it.
- **Heartbeat counting.** Schandry accuracy median .70. It is unrelated to BCAT threshold, MAIA, GERT accuracy and BAT.

## 1c. Body vs awareness, and H2B robustness (2026-10-06)

**Body vs awareness** (`Results/body_vs_awareness/report.md`; 224 participants, belt follows pacer):
Numbers below use the four-cycle breathing measure (1f).
- **No direct bodily route.** The breathing change actually made does not predict perceived intensity
  (per SD: 0.013 [-0.007, 0.034]; equivalent to zero within +-0.05 scale points).
- **Awareness is weak at clip level.** b = .055, p = .067.
- **Felt arousal predicts intensity.** b = .072, p < .001.
- **The body acts through a chain.** Bodily change -> detection (b = .43, p < .001) -> felt arousal
  (p = .007) -> intensity; serial indirect effect .0031 [.0007, .0065].

**H2B robustness** (`Results/h2b_robustness/report.md`):
- **Replicates on independent clips.** The combined task's 60 clips (none shared with baseline):
  r = -.176, p = .002.
- **Survives controls.** Response bias, confidence, MAIA, demographics, PHQ-4 and burnout leave
  beta = -.138, p = .028.
- **Robust to measurement checks.** It holds under rank and robust estimation, and without the ceiling
  thresholds or influential points.
- **Holds in the belt-compliant subsample.** beta = -.13, p = .04 unadjusted; with compliance controls
  beta = -.095, p = .18 (four-cycle entrainment gain; it was -.13, p = .08 with the cycle-3 gain).
- **Thresholds are of modest reliability.** Spearman-Brown .49; disattenuated r about -.25. They are well calibrated.

## 1d. Equivalence tests and clip-level models (2026-10-06)

**Equivalence** (`Results/equivalence/report.md`; SESOI |r| = .17, i.e. H2B; 0.05 intensity points; 2 accuracy points; 1 bpm):
- **Equivalent to zero:**
  - salience and direction -> intensity;
  - the breathing change made -> intensity;
  - salience and direction -> heart rate;
  - H1A, H1B, H2A, H2C and E1A.
- **Inconclusive:**
  - salience x direction -> intensity (equivalent at 0.10 only);
  - direction -> accuracy (-1.1 points);
  - burnout -> resting HR and HRV (cannot exclude |r| up to about .2). The draft's "perception, not
    production" claim is softened accordingly.

**Clip level** (`Results/clip_level_models/report.md`):
- **H3B holds.** b = .035, p = .045; BH p = .16.
- **The awareness effect holds.** b = .045, p = .029.
- **E2C between-person is robust.** b = .079, p = .024.
- **E2C within-person is fragile.** It falls from .077 to .054 with clip random intercepts, and to .033 (p = .16)
  with by-participant slopes. The participant slopes vary widely (SD .21).
- **Body -> awareness -> arousal -> intensity chain under random slopes:**
  - the first two links hold;
  - the arousal -> intensity link is marginal (b = .051, p = .057);
  - the serial indirect effect is borderline.

## 1g. Burnout and entrainment (2026-10-07)

This answers Fukuda's thesis comments: does burnout dampen the body's response, or only the felt sense of it?
(`Results/burnout_entrainment/report.md`; 224 participants whose belt followed the pacer)
- **The body responds equally.** Burnout is unrelated to entrainment gain (r = .01; equivalent to zero at
  |r| = .17) or to direction compliance (r = -.04; equivalent). At trial level, burnout x prescribed change is 0.002 per
  SD [-0.019, 0.023] against a gain of 0.87 (combined task). It is -0.004 [-0.018, 0.010] at baseline.
- **Felt arousal is lower.** Burnout still predicts lower mean felt arousal with gain and compliance held
  constant (beta = -.16, p = .016). Entrainment gain itself is unrelated to felt arousal (r = .07).
- **Responsiveness is intact.** The arousal response to the breathing change is not blunted. If anything it is
  slightly larger with higher burnout (p = .037 continuous; p = .081 with direction coding), but this is fragile.
- **Burnout range.** BAT-23 total M = 69.5, SD = 14.7, range 31-110 against a possible 23-115. Range restriction is
  unlikely. Mean item score 3.0; compare with the BAT manual's cut-offs before writing.
- Caveat: per-participant gain has low split-half reliability (.36), so the participant-level r is attenuated. The
  trial-level interaction test does not depend on it.

## 3. Manuscript structure: candidate story

The thesis is framed around burnout, and its burnout hypotheses are null. The data support a
stronger paper framed around **interoception and emotion perception**, with burnout as a
secondary between-person finding. Proposed spine:

1. **The manipulation works, in the body and in experience.** Preregistered R1 and R2A
   replicate: acceleration feels more arousing, and salience increases detection.
   *Physio adds:* breathing actually followed the pacer, and the size of the actual change
   predicts detection and felt arousal. Reviewers will ask for this first.
2. **Two routes from body to perceiving others** (a dissociation):
   - *Sensitivity -> accuracy:* people with lower breath-change thresholds recognise emotions
     more accurately (H2B, preregistered, survives BH). Felt arousal does not predict accuracy.
   - *Felt state -> intensity:* blocks with more felt arousal, or with detected changes, are
     followed by more intense perceptions of others' emotions (E2C, preregistered exploratory).
     Thresholds do not predict intensity.
3. **Awareness is necessary but not sufficient.** Making changes more salient raised detection
   and amplified felt arousal. It did not move perceived intensity, and the CI is tight. This
   honest null bounds the effect and argues against a simple account in which more awareness
   gives more intensity.
4. **Burnout blunts felt arousal, not perception** (between-person, r = -.20). *Physio decides*
   whether this is blunted physiology or blunted perception of it. The thesis says this
   cannot be resolved with self-report, and the HR and breathing data can resolve it.

The strongest new contribution comes from step 1, the part of step 2 that physiology can test,
and step 4. Each separates **objective bodily change** from **awareness of change** and from
**felt arousal**. That is the question the title promises ("breath awareness and change"), and
neither the thesis nor the reanalysis could answer it without physiology.
